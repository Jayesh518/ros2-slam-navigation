# Phase 4: planner and controller comparison

What was run: the baseline (NavFn planner, MPPI controller, Nav2 1.3.13 shipped defaults with robot-specific edits only) and four variants that each change one component: **smac2d** (SmacPlanner2D), **thetastar** (Theta*), **dwb** (DWB controller), **rpp** (Regulated Pure Pursuit controller). 20 start/goal pairs per world, 1 trial per pair, four worlds: A1 open room, B1 office, C1 pillar grid (the original design) and D dense random clutter (added after the baseline, protocol change log (l)). 5 configurations x 4 worlds x 20 = **400 trials**, all valid (no failed resets), **0 collisions in any trial**. The custom recovery configuration (`recovery`, the sixth configuration) is not built yet (Phase 6). Raw rows: `results/trials/*.csv`. Tables: `results/summary.md`, `results/paired_vs_baseline.md` (generated by `scripts/analyse.py`). Everything below is traceable to those files.

## Decision rule, applied exactly as pre-registered (protocol section 8)
Unit = start/goal pair, paired against the baseline, 95% bootstrap interval over pairs (10,000 resamples, fixed seed). Success: real if the mean difference is at least 10 percentage points and the interval excludes zero. Time and path length: pairs where both trials succeeded; real if the interval excludes zero and the size exceeds 5% of the baseline value. Four primary comparisons (one per variant), each reported for the original design (A to C, 60 pairs) and with the post-hoc world D (A to D, 80 pairs); intervals are not adjusted for multiple comparisons.

![Paired differences to the baseline](figures/phase4-paired-differences.png)

In words:
- **Success:** no variant changed success clearly, except rpp in the original design (A to C), which lost 10 points and just meets the rule; with world D added the same drop is 7 points and does not. The shaded band in the figure is the zone below the 10-point rule.
- **Smac2D and Theta\* find shorter and faster routes** than NavFn: about 0.7 to 0.8 m (about 7% of the 10.8 m average baseline path) shorter and 6 to 7 s faster over the pairs both configurations solved. This is a reproducible effect in every world (per-world rows are suggestive only).
- **RPP is slower** by about 4 s; DWB is indistinguishable from the baseline in time and path.
- Per-world rows (`results/paired_vs_baseline.md`) are labelled suggestive: for example smac2d and rpp in world B lose 5 of 20 pairs (diff -0.25), and thetastar gains 3 (diff +0.15), but each rests on 20 pairs.

## Hypotheses (fixed in the protocol before any trial)
- **H1** (planner choice changes success little in the open world A but more in the cluttered world C): **not supported.** Planner choice changed success by 0 in both A (20/20 for all three planners) and C (20/20). Where planners did differ in success is world B, which H1 did not name (smac2d 11/20, thetastar 19/20, NavFn 16/20; suggestive, 20 pairs). Planner choice did change time and path length in every world.
- **H2** (the custom recovery raises success in the cluttered world, at a cost in time): **not tested.** The custom recovery behaviour does not exist yet (Phase 6), and the baseline is already 20/20 in C1 and in D, so no improvement is possible there; the only world with failures to remove is B.
- **H3** (localisation error is highest in the most symmetric world, with failures there mostly from localisation): **not supported.** There were no failures at all in C1 for any configuration, and the baseline's mean AMCL error in C1 (0.13 m) is about the same as in A1 (0.13 m) and below B1 (0.17 m). The localisation problem showed up in B1 instead (next section).

## What stands out (for Phase 5 to explain; descriptions, not tested claims)
- **Localisation fails in the office for DWB and RPP.** In world B, mean AMCL error is 0.42 m (dwb) and 0.46 m (rpp) against 0.17 m (baseline), 0.22 m (smac2d), 0.12 m (thetastar). AMCL error exceeded 0.5 m near the end of 7 of dwb's 9 failures and 9 of rpp's 9 failures. Baseline failures in B were mixed (2 of 4 with large AMCL error), and smac2d's were mostly aborts (8 of 9) with large AMCL error in only 4 trials. Possible mechanism: how these controllers rotate and move near doorways changes what the LiDAR sees; not yet checked.
- **Smac2D aborts in the office** (8 of 9 failures are Nav2 aborts): consistent with the planner failing to find or keep a path through the 1 m doorways, possibly an interaction with the costmap inflation and the shipped planner tolerance; not yet checked.
- **Theta\* did best** in the only world with room to improve (19 of 20 in B), with the lowest AMCL error there (0.12 m).
- **RPP lost one trial in world A** by ground truth although Nav2 reported success (a goal-not-reached case), a reminder that the success rule has two parts.
- Collisions: 0 in 400 trials, so the metric does not separate any configuration in these worlds.

## Confounds and limits (all four are real)
1. **Time and path are conditional on success.** They use only pairs both configurations solved; in world B the planners that fail more (smac2d) are measured on the easier pairs, and the effect on path length may be partly a selection effect. The success comparison has no such selection.
2. **Order and drift.** Sessions run serially, one configuration after another (baseline A to C earlier in the day, variants later), at the same real-time factor; within a session trials are in randomised order, but configurations are not interleaved, so a slow drift of the machine over hours would be confounded with configuration. Repeat spread in validation was about 2% in time, so a large drift is unlikely but not excluded.
3. **Defaults against defaults.** DWB and RPP use the standard Nav2 default parameter sets with only the speed limits set to the robot's; nothing was tuned. The comparison says how these defaults behave on this robot, not which controller is best when tuned.
4. **World D is post-hoc and added no headroom** (the baseline scored 20/20 there). The A to C columns are the original design; A to D is reported next to it and labelled.
5. **Small samples where the signal is.** World B carries almost all success differences and has 20 pairs; per-world results are suggestive only and intervals are wide (for example 0.34 to 0.74 for 11 of 20).
6. **Four primary comparisons, intervals not adjusted.** Simulation only; maps from default SLAM Toolbox (rotated 0.6 to 2.4 degrees, unobserved patches); 3.5 m LiDAR; Gaussian LiDAR noise 0.01 m; AMCL and Nav2 not seedable, so re-running gives close but not identical numbers.
